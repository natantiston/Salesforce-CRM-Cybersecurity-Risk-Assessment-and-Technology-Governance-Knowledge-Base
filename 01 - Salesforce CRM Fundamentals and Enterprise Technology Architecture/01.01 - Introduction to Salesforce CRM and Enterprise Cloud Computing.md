# 01.01 - Introduction to Salesforce CRM and Enterprise Cloud Computing

## Part 1 - Foundations, Concepts and Technology Architecture

### 1. Introduction to Salesforce CRM

Salesforce is a cloud-based Customer Relationship Management (CRM) platform that enables organizations to manage customer relationships, sales activities, service operations, marketing processes, customer information and digital engagement through a centralized technology ecosystem.

Unlike traditional CRM applications that organizations install and maintain entirely within their own data centers, Salesforce operates primarily through a Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) delivery model. Customers access the platform through the internet, while Salesforce operates and maintains the underlying cloud infrastructure and platform services.

Salesforce has evolved beyond traditional customer relationship management. It now provides a broad enterprise technology ecosystem that includes application development, data management, artificial intelligence, workflow automation, integration services, analytics and customer engagement capabilities.

Organizations use Salesforce to support business processes such as:

- Customer registration and account management.
- Sales opportunity and pipeline management.
- Customer service and support operations.
- Marketing campaign management.
- E-commerce and digital customer engagement.
- Customer data management and analytics.
- Workflow automation and business process orchestration.
- AI-powered customer interactions and business assistance.
- Integration with enterprise resource planning (ERP) systems.
- Customer identity, authentication and digital service delivery.

From a cybersecurity and governance perspective, Salesforce must be treated as a critical enterprise application platform rather than simply a customer database.

It can process sensitive customer information, commercially confidential data, personal information, transaction records and business-critical operational information. Its availability, integrity and confidentiality can directly affect business operations, regulatory compliance and customer trust.

Consequently, protecting Salesforce requires an understanding of its underlying technology, application architecture, data flows, integrations, identities, security configurations and operational dependencies.

### 2. Evolution of Salesforce and Enterprise CRM

Traditional CRM systems were originally designed to centralize customer information and support sales activities. Organizations commonly deployed these systems within their own infrastructure, requiring dedicated servers, databases, application software and internal IT resources.

These deployments created several operational responsibilities:

- Procuring and maintaining physical infrastructure.
- Installing and upgrading application software.
- Managing database infrastructure.
- Implementing network security.
- Maintaining application availability.
- Managing backups and disaster recovery.
- Applying security patches and configuration changes.
- Supporting application integrations.

The introduction of cloud computing changed this model.

Salesforce became one of the major providers of cloud-based CRM services, allowing organizations to consume CRM functionality through a subscription-based service rather than operating the entire application infrastructure themselves.

This transition introduced a different operating model:

| Traditional on-premises CRM                                                         | Cloud-based Salesforce CRM                                                                   |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Organization operates application infrastructure.                                   | Salesforce operates the underlying SaaS infrastructure.                                      |
| Internal IT manages servers and databases.                                          | Salesforce manages the underlying platform infrastructure.                                   |
| Organization controls application installation and upgrades.                        | Salesforce manages platform releases and service updates.                                    |
| Organization manages infrastructure availability.                                   | Salesforce provides the underlying service availability commitments.                         |
| Internal IT manages infrastructure-level maintenance.                               | Responsibilities are divided between Salesforce and the customer.                            |
| Security is implemented across internally operated infrastructure and applications. | Security responsibilities are shared across Salesforce, the customer and external providers. |

This transition does not eliminate the organization's cybersecurity responsibilities.

Instead, it changes where those responsibilities exist and how they must be governed.

For example, Salesforce may operate the underlying cloud infrastructure, but the customer remains responsible for appropriately configuring user permissions, controlling access to customer records, managing integrations, protecting credentials and establishing suitable security governance.

This distinction is fundamental to Salesforce cybersecurity risk management.

### 3. Understanding Salesforce as a Software-as-a-Service Platform

Software as a Service (SaaS) is a cloud computing delivery model in which a provider makes an application available to customers over a network, generally through a subscription-based commercial arrangement.

The provider operates the underlying service infrastructure and maintains the application environment.

Customers consume the service without having to independently install and maintain the complete application stack.

Salesforce CRM is primarily delivered through this model.

A typical Salesforce SaaS environment involves three major responsibility areas:

1\. Salesforce service provider

Salesforce operates and maintains the underlying service infrastructure and platform according to the applicable product architecture and contractual commitments.

2\. Customer organization

The customer configures and administers its Salesforce environment, manages users and permissions, governs its business data, controls integrations and implements appropriate organizational security requirements.

3\. Third-party technology providers

External providers may supply connected applications, middleware, identity services, AI models, payment services, analytics platforms and other technologies that interact with Salesforce.

These parties introduce additional security dependencies that must be evaluated.

#### 3.1 SaaS Service Characteristics

The Salesforce SaaS model generally provides the following characteristics:

| Characteristic                   | Description                                                                                                                                          |
| -------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Subscription-based consumption   | Organizations subscribe to Salesforce services and applicable products.                                                                              |
| Internet-based access            | Authorized users access the platform through supported network connections.                                                                          |
| Provider-managed infrastructure  | Salesforce operates the underlying infrastructure supporting its services.                                                                           |
| Shared platform services         | Customers consume services running on Salesforce-managed infrastructure.                                                                             |
| Centralized platform maintenance | Salesforce manages applicable platform maintenance and releases.                                                                                     |
| Configurable applications        | Customers configure applications and business processes according to their requirements.                                                             |
| Integration capabilities         | Salesforce supports connections with external applications and enterprise systems.                                                                   |
| Service scalability              | Organizations can use available platform capabilities to support changing business requirements, subject to product limits and service architecture. |

#### 3.2 SaaS Security Implications

The SaaS model introduces specific cybersecurity considerations.

First, the customer does not have unrestricted administrative access to the underlying physical infrastructure or operating systems supporting Salesforce.

Second, the organization must understand which security controls are operated by Salesforce and which must be configured and maintained by the customer.

Third, the organization depends on the provider's service availability, security architecture, operational controls and contractual commitments.

Fourth, integrations with external systems can extend the Salesforce attack surface beyond the Salesforce platform itself.

For example, an organization may operate Salesforce CRM with connections to an e-commerce platform, SAP S/4HANA ERP and an external payment service.

Although Salesforce is delivered as SaaS, the overall enterprise solution consists of multiple interconnected technology environments.

A security weakness in an external integration could therefore affect the confidentiality, integrity or availability of information processed by Salesforce.

### 4. Cloud Computing Fundamentals and Service Delivery Models

Cloud computing is the delivery of computing resources and services through network-accessible infrastructure rather than requiring organizations to operate all computing resources locally.

Cloud services can include:

- Computing and processing resources.
- Storage and database services.
- Networking and connectivity.
- Application hosting.
- Development platforms.
- Security services.
- Analytics and artificial intelligence.
- Business applications.

Cloud computing is commonly divided into three primary service delivery models: Infrastructure as a Service (IaaS), Platform as a Service (PaaS) and Software as a Service (SaaS).

#### 4.1 Infrastructure as a Service (IaaS)

IaaS provides virtualized infrastructure resources through a cloud provider.

The customer typically manages operating systems, applications, middleware and relevant configurations, while the provider manages the underlying physical infrastructure and virtualization services.

Examples include:

- Amazon Web Services (AWS) EC2.
- Microsoft Azure Virtual Machines.
- Google Cloud Compute Engine.

Security responsibilities:

Organizations must manage operating system hardening, application security, identity permissions, network configurations, vulnerability remediation and data protection according to the service model.

#### 4.2 Platform as a Service (PaaS)

PaaS provides an environment for developing, deploying and operating applications without requiring customers to manage the complete underlying infrastructure.

The provider manages the platform infrastructure and applicable runtime services, while customers remain responsible for their applications, code, data and configurations according to the platform's responsibility model.

Examples include:

- Salesforce Platform.
- Microsoft Azure App Service.
- Google App Engine.

Salesforce provides platform capabilities that allow organizations to create custom applications, configure business processes, develop Apex code and build Lightning components.

#### 4.3 Software as a Service (SaaS)

SaaS provides complete application services that customers access through supported interfaces.

Examples include:

- Salesforce CRM.
- Microsoft 365.
- ServiceNow.
- Google Workspace.

In the SaaS model, the provider operates the underlying application service and infrastructure. Customers configure the application and manage their own data, identities, permissions and business processes.

#### 4.4 Comparison of Cloud Service Models

| Responsibility            | IaaS     | PaaS     | SaaS                                                         |
| ------------------------- | -------- | -------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Physical infrastructure   | Provider | Provider | Provider                                                     |
| Virtualization            | Provider | Provider | Provider                                                     |
| Operating system          | Customer | Provider | Provider                                                     |
| Runtime and middleware    | Customer | Provider | Provider                                                     |
| Application               | Customer | Customer | Provider                                                     |
| Application configuration | Customer | Customer | Shared, depending on service                                 |
| Data governance           | Customer | Customer | Customer responsibility with provider-supported capabilities |
| User access management    | Customer | Customer | Shared, with customer-managed access configuration           |

The exact allocation of responsibilities varies by provider and service.

#### 4.5 Why This Matters for Salesforce Security

Understanding the cloud service model is essential when conducting Salesforce cybersecurity risk assessments.

An organization should not assume that because Salesforce manages the infrastructure, all security responsibilities are automatically covered.

For example:

- Salesforce manages the underlying platform infrastructure.
- The customer configures Salesforce users and permissions.
- The customer governs the information stored in its Salesforce organization.
- The customer secures custom-developed applications and integrations.
- The customer evaluates external applications connected to Salesforce.
- Both parties have responsibilities during security incidents, according to their respective contractual and operational obligations.

Salesforce's Well-Architected Framework identifies Trust, Reliability, Operational Excellence, Resource and Cost Optimization, and Fairness as its five architectural pillars. These provide a useful foundation for evaluating the quality and security of Salesforce solutions.&#x20;

### 5. Salesforce Platform Overview and Core Technology Components

Salesforce is a configurable cloud platform that combines application functionality, data management, automation, development capabilities and integration services.

An enterprise Salesforce implementation can contain multiple interconnected components.

#### 5.1 Salesforce Organization (Org)

A Salesforce organization, commonly called an org, is the logical environment in which an organization operates its Salesforce applications, data, users, configurations and customizations.

An org contains components such as:

- User accounts.
- Profiles and permission sets.
- Business objects and records.
- Applications and application configurations.
- Custom fields and objects.
- Automation workflows.
- Apex code.
- Lightning components.
- Security configurations.
- Integration configurations.

From a cybersecurity perspective, the org is an important unit of security administration and assessment.

An organization must establish appropriate controls over its org configuration, users, data, applications and external connections.

#### 5.2 Salesforce Database and Data Model

Salesforce uses a platform-managed data architecture that organizes business information into objects, fields and records.

The primary concepts are:

| Component     | Description                                                          | Example                     |
| ------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Object        | Structure that stores a particular category of business information. | Account                     |
| Field         | Attribute associated with an object.                                 | Account Name                |
| Record        | Individual data entry within an object.                              | A specific customer account |
| Relationship  | Association between objects.                                         | Contact linked to Account   |
| Custom Object | Customer-created business data structure.                            | Customer_Contract__c        |
| Custom Field  | Customer-created data attribute.                                     | Customer_ID__c              |

Salesforce provides standard objects, such as Account, Contact, Opportunity, Case and Lead, alongside custom objects created to support organization-specific requirements.

Security relevance:

A poorly designed data model can introduce security and governance weaknesses.

Examples include:

- Storing sensitive information in fields without appropriate access restrictions.
- Creating custom objects without considering data classification.
- Allowing excessive record access through sharing configurations.
- Storing authentication credentials in inappropriate fields.
- Exposing sensitive customer information through reports or dashboards.
- Retaining personal information longer than necessary.

Data architecture must therefore be considered during cybersecurity assessments, not treated solely as an application development concern.

#### 5.3 Salesforce Application Layer

The application layer contains the business functionality through which users interact with Salesforce.

Examples include:

- Sales processes.
- Customer service workflows.
- Marketing activities.
- Customer account management.
- Case management.
- Business automation.
- Custom business applications.

Applications can use standard Salesforce functionality or custom-developed components.

The security assessment must consider how these applications process information, enforce permissions and interact with other platform components.

#### 5.4 Salesforce Automation and Business Logic

Salesforce supports automation capabilities that allow organizations to execute business processes without requiring every action to be performed manually.

Examples include:

- Salesforce Flow.
- Approval Processes.
- Validation Rules.
- Apex Triggers.
- Scheduled automation.
- Event-driven processing.

These capabilities introduce additional security considerations.

For example, an automated process may access customer records, modify account information, initiate business actions or communicate with external systems.

Security reviews should evaluate:

- Which user or execution context runs the automation.
- What records and fields it can access.
- Whether the automation enforces appropriate permissions.
- Whether input validation is implemented.
- Whether its actions are logged.
- Whether changes are authorized and tested.

#### 5.5 Salesforce Development and Customization

Salesforce provides development capabilities that enable organizations to customize the platform.

Important technologies include:

| Technology               | Purpose                                                          |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Apex                     | Salesforce server-side programming language.                     |
| Lightning Web Components | Framework for developing user interface components.              |
| Visualforce              | Technology for developing customized Salesforce pages.           |
| Salesforce APIs          | Interfaces for connecting Salesforce with external applications. |
| Salesforce Metadata      | Definitions and configurations of platform components.           |
| Salesforce Flow          | Declarative automation and business process execution.           |

Custom development increases the organization's responsibility for application security.

For example, insecure Apex code may introduce SOQL injection, authorization weaknesses or inappropriate access to sensitive records.

Custom Lightning components may also introduce client-side security weaknesses if data is improperly handled or exposed.

These risks must be addressed through secure development practices, code reviews and appropriate security testing.

### 7. Enterprise CRM Architecture and Business Process Integration

Salesforce is frequently implemented as part of a larger enterprise application environment.

It may act as the central customer relationship platform while relying on other applications to execute business processes.

For example, a telecommunications company may use Salesforce to manage customer interactions while relying on SAP S/4HANA for enterprise resource planning, an e-commerce platform for online purchases and separate operational systems for service provisioning.

This means that Salesforce security cannot be evaluated independently of the surrounding enterprise architecture.

#### 7.1 Typical Enterprise Application Architecture

A conceptual enterprise CRM environment may contain the following components:

| Layer                | Technology or application                | Business function                           |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Customer interaction | Website, mobile app, customer portal     | Customer engagement                         |
| CRM application      | Salesforce Sales Cloud and Service Cloud | Customer relationship management            |
| Customer data        | Salesforce Data Cloud                    | Customer data unification                   |
| Integration          | MuleSoft and API services                | Enterprise connectivity                     |
| ERP                  | SAP S/4HANA                              | Finance, inventory and enterprise resources |
| E-commerce           | Digital commerce platform                | Online transactions                         |
| Identity             | Enterprise identity provider             | Authentication and identity federation      |
| AI                   | Agentforce and external LLMs             | AI-powered interactions                     |
| Security operations  | SIEM, XDR and SOAR                       | Monitoring and incident response            |

#### 7.2 Example: Customer Registration Process

Consider a customer registering through an organization's website.

A simplified process may involve:

1. The customer accesses the website.
2. The customer submits registration information.
3. The website sends the information to an API integration layer.
4. The integration layer validates and processes the request.
5. Salesforce creates or updates the customer record.
6. The CRM application associates the customer with relevant business processes.
7. Customer information may be synchronized with other enterprise applications.
8. Security monitoring systems collect available events from the connected environments.

Each step introduces potential security considerations.

For example, if the API does not properly authenticate requests or validate submitted information, an attacker may attempt to manipulate customer records or submit unauthorized transactions.

#### 7.3 Example: Customer Order Processing

A second example involves an online customer purchase.

1. The customer accesses the e-commerce platform.
2. The customer selects a product or service.
3. The e-commerce application submits the order information.
4. The integration layer communicates with Salesforce.
5. Salesforce updates the customer and order-related business records.
6. SAP S/4HANA processes relevant ERP activities.
7. The order status is synchronized with connected applications.
8. Salesforce may provide customer service visibility into the transaction.

Security assessments should examine the integrity of order information, authentication of integration services, API permissions, data confidentiality and transaction monitoring.

#### 7.4 Security Implications of Enterprise Integration

Enterprise integration increases the number of trust relationships and potential attack paths.

Consider the following scenarios:

| Integration                     | Potential security risk                                               |
| ------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Salesforce to SAP S/4HANA       | Unauthorized modification of business or customer-related information |
| Salesforce to e-commerce        | Manipulation of customer or order data                                |
| Salesforce to payment gateway   | Exposure or misuse of payment-related information                     |
| Salesforce to customer portal   | Unauthorized access to customer records                               |
| Salesforce to identity provider | Account compromise or authentication failure                          |
| Salesforce to external LLM      | Sensitive data disclosure                                             |
| Salesforce to SIEM              | Incomplete security event visibility                                  |
| Salesforce to third-party SaaS  | Vendor compromise or unauthorized data processing                     |

A complete Salesforce cybersecurity assessment must therefore consider both the internal Salesforce environment and its external technology dependencies.

### 8. Salesforce Data Architecture and Information Lifecycle

Data is one of the most important assets within a Salesforce environment.

Organizations use Salesforce to store, process and exchange information that supports customer relationships and business operations.

A security assessment must consider the entire information lifecycle.

#### 8.1 Data Lifecycle

A typical data lifecycle consists of:

1. Data collection.
2. Data ingestion.
3. Data validation.
4. Data processing.
5. Data storage.
6. Data access and usage.
7. Data sharing and transmission.
8. Data retention.
9. Data archival.
10. Data deletion or disposal.

Each stage introduces different security and privacy requirements.

For example, collecting unnecessary personal information increases privacy exposure, while retaining sensitive information beyond its required retention period increases the consequences of a potential data breach.

#### 8.2 Data Classification

Organizations should establish a data classification model to determine the appropriate protection requirements for information processed through Salesforce.

An illustrative classification model is:

| Classification | Description                                   | Example                                        | Protection requirements                                                                  |
| -------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Public         | Information approved for public disclosure    | Public product information                     | Appropriate integrity and availability controls                                          |
| Internal       | Information intended for authorized employees | Internal business notes                        | Authenticated access and appropriate permissions                                         |
| Confidential   | Sensitive commercial or customer information  | Customer financial information                 | Restricted access, encryption where appropriate and monitoring                           |
| Restricted     | Highly sensitive or regulated information     | Health information and sensitive identity data | Strong access restrictions, appropriate encryption, auditability and enhanced monitoring |

This classification model is an enterprise reference example, not a mandatory Salesforce classification scheme.

#### 8.3 Data Security Considerations

Important considerations include:

- Data ownership and accountability.
- Data classification.
- Data minimization.
- Access authorization.
- Encryption in transit and at rest.
- Data retention and deletion.
- Data residency requirements.
- Data sharing with external providers.
- Data loss prevention.
- Privacy and consent management.
- Data backup and recovery.
- Data integrity and auditability.

Salesforce provides platform capabilities that support several of these requirements, but the customer must determine how to configure and govern them.

#### 8.4 Data Flows and Trust Boundaries

A trust boundary represents a point where information crosses between different security domains, systems, applications or privilege levels.

Examples include:

- Customer browser to Salesforce Experience Cloud.
- Salesforce to MuleSoft.
- MuleSoft to SAP S/4HANA.
- Salesforce to an external LLM.
- Salesforce to a payment provider.
- Salesforce to an enterprise SIEM.
- Salesforce to a third-party application.

Every trust boundary should be assessed for authentication, authorization, encryption, data validation, logging and monitoring.

This is especially important when an integration transfers customer information or allows one system to initiate actions in another.

### 9. Enterprise Cloud Computing Security and Shared Responsibility

One of the most important concepts in Salesforce cybersecurity is the Shared Responsibility Model.

In cloud computing, the provider and customer have different security responsibilities. These responsibilities depend on the service model, product architecture, contractual commitments and specific deployment.

Salesforce's Well-Architected Trust guidance describes this relationship as Salesforce securing the platform while customers design and secure the solutions they build on it.&#x20;

Salesforce Architects+1



#### 9.1 Salesforce Responsibilities

Salesforce is responsible for applicable underlying platform and infrastructure security controls, including:

- Physical data center security.
- Infrastructure protection.
- Platform infrastructure maintenance.
- Operating system and infrastructure security.
- Platform vulnerability remediation.
- Infrastructure-level network security.
- Platform availability and resilience.
- Applicable platform security certifications and attestations.
- Tenant isolation mechanisms.

The exact responsibilities and control coverage depend on the Salesforce service and its contractual and technical scope.

#### 9.2 Customer Responsibilities

The customer organization remains responsible for securing its Salesforce implementation.

Important responsibilities include:

- User identity and access management.
- User provisioning and deprovisioning.
- Authentication and authorization configurations.
- Permission sets and profiles.
- Record-level and field-level access.
- Data classification and governance.
- Application configuration.
- Custom code security.
- API authentication and authorization.
- Integration security.
- Third-party application assessments.
- Security monitoring and incident response.
- Backup and recovery strategy.
- Compliance and privacy obligations.

#### 9.3 Shared Responsibilities

Some security activities require coordination between Salesforce and the customer.

Examples include:

| Security activity                 | Salesforce responsibility                                     | Customer responsibility                                               |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Infrastructure security           | Operates underlying infrastructure controls                   | Reviews applicable assurance information                              |
| Platform vulnerability management | Remediates platform-level vulnerabilities                     | Remediates customer-developed code and configuration weaknesses       |
| Identity management               | Provides identity and access capabilities                     | Configures and governs identities and permissions                     |
| Data protection                   | Provides platform security capabilities                       | Classifies, governs and appropriately protects customer data          |
| Security monitoring               | Provides applicable platform security events and capabilities | Configures monitoring and investigates relevant activity              |
| Incident response                 | Handles incidents affecting Salesforce services               | Responds to incidents involving its org, users, integrations and data |
| Compliance                        | Provides applicable certifications and assurance              | Demonstrates compliance of its own implementation and operations      |

#### 9.4 Why Shared Responsibility Matters for Risk Assessment

A common mistake in cloud security is assuming that the cloud provider is responsible for every security control.

This creates a potential governance gap.

For example, Salesforce may provide the platform infrastructure and security capabilities, but an organization might configure overly permissive access to customer records.

The infrastructure may be operating securely while the customer's Salesforce implementation remains exposed.

A cybersecurity risk assessment must therefore identify:

1. Which security controls are provided by Salesforce.
2. Which controls require customer configuration.
3. Which controls depend on external technology providers.
4. Which controls require collaboration.
5. Who owns each control.
6. What evidence demonstrates that each control operates effectively.

The shared responsibility model must be documented and communicated to the relevant business, technology, security and compliance stakeholders.

### 11. Salesforce Technology Dependencies, Limitations and Security Considerations

Although Salesforce provides extensive platform capabilities, enterprise implementations depend on several technical and operational factors.

Understanding these dependencies is essential when evaluating the overall security posture.

#### 11.1 Platform Dependencies

A Salesforce implementation may depend on:

- Salesforce service availability.
- Internet connectivity.
- DNS infrastructure.
- Enterprise identity providers.
- API gateways.
- Middleware.
- External databases.
- Third-party SaaS applications.
- AI service providers.
- Enterprise security platforms.

An outage or compromise affecting one of these dependencies may disrupt Salesforce business processes even if the Salesforce platform itself remains operational.

#### 11.2 SaaS Control Limitations

Customers do not have the same infrastructure-level control over a SaaS environment as they would over an internally managed application.

For example, customers generally cannot independently administer the underlying Salesforce production operating systems or directly manage the physical infrastructure.

Instead, they must work within the security, configuration, integration and monitoring capabilities exposed by Salesforce.

This creates several assessment considerations:

- Availability of required security logs.
- Access to platform-level security evidence.
- Product licensing requirements.
- Configurable security capabilities.
- API and integration limitations.
- Data retention capabilities.
- Backup and recovery dependencies.
- Provider contractual commitments.

#### 11.3 Product and Licensing Dependencies

Not every Salesforce security capability is automatically included in every Salesforce subscription.

Some features require additional licenses, product editions, add-ons or specific configurations.

Examples include certain Salesforce Shield capabilities, advanced monitoring functions and specialized security services.

Therefore, during a cybersecurity assessment, the assessor should verify:

1. Which Salesforce products the organization actually uses.
2. Which licenses and subscriptions are active.
3. Which security capabilities are available.
4. Which capabilities have been enabled.
5. Which capabilities have been properly configured.
6. Which controls are dependent on external products.

A security recommendation that assumes an unavailable license is not an effective remediation plan.

#### 11.4 Architectural Complexity

Enterprise Salesforce environments can become complex because of multiple applications, customizations, integrations and business units.

For example, an organization may have:

- Multiple Salesforce orgs.
- Several integration environments.
- Different identity providers.
- Multiple external SaaS applications.
- Several AI services.
- Different data retention requirements.
- Separate development and production environments.

This complexity can introduce inconsistent security configurations and governance gaps.

Organizations should maintain an accurate inventory of Salesforce environments, applications, integrations, identities, data assets and security controls.

#### 12.2 Example Business Process

4. The e-commerce application sends transaction information through an API integration layer.
5. MuleSoft processes the integration request.
6. Salesforce receives relevant customer and order information.
7. Salesforce updates the appropriate CRM records.
8. SAP S/4HANA processes relevant ERP activities.
9. The customer receives transaction updates through the applicable customer channel.
